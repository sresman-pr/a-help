Being involved in a car accident can be a traumatic and disorienting experience. In the moments following a collision, it can be difficult to think clearly about what you should do next. However, the actions you take immediately after an accident can significantly impact your ability to recover compensation for your injuries.

Here's a comprehensive guide on what to do if you're involved in a car accident in Maryland.

## 1. Check for Injuries and Call 911

Your first priority should always be safety. Check yourself and your passengers for injuries. If anyone is hurt, call 911 immediately. Even if injuries seem minor, it's important to have emergency responders assess the situation.

**Important:** Don't move anyone who appears seriously injured unless there's an immediate danger (like a fire). Moving someone with spinal injuries can cause further harm.

## 2. Move to Safety If Possible

If your vehicle is drivable and it's safe to do so, move it to the side of the road to avoid blocking traffic and prevent additional accidents. Turn on your hazard lights to alert other drivers.

If you can't move your vehicle, stay inside with your seatbelt fastened until help arrives, especially if you're on a busy highway.

## 3. Document the Accident Scene

Once everyone is safe, start documenting the accident scene. This evidence can be crucial for your insurance claim and any potential legal action:

- **Take photos** of all vehicles involved, showing damage from multiple angles
- **Photograph the accident scene**, including road conditions, traffic signs, and any debris
- **Document your injuries** with photos
- **Note weather conditions** and time of day
- **Look for traffic cameras** or businesses with security cameras that may have captured the accident

## 4. Exchange Information with Other Drivers

Maryland law requires drivers involved in accidents to exchange information. Get the following from all other drivers involved:

- Full name and contact information
- Driver's license number
- Insurance company and policy number
- Vehicle registration and license plate number
- Make, model, and color of the vehicle

**Important:** Be polite but avoid discussing fault or apologizing for the accident. Even a simple "I'm sorry" can be used against you later.

## 5. Get Witness Information

If there are any witnesses to the accident, ask for their names and contact information. Witness testimony can be invaluable in establishing what happened, especially if there's a dispute about fault.

## 6. File a Police Report

In Maryland, you're required to report accidents that involve injuries, death, or property damage exceeding $3,000. Even if the damage seems minor, it's generally a good idea to file a police report. The report creates an official record of the accident and can be helpful evidence for your claim.

Wait for the police to arrive, and provide them with accurate information about what happened. Ask the responding officer for the report number so you can obtain a copy later.

## 7. Seek Medical Attention

Even if you feel fine, see a doctor as soon as possible after the accident. Some injuries, like whiplash, concussions, or internal injuries, may not be immediately apparent but can become serious if left untreated.

Additionally, your medical records create a documented link between the accident and your injuries, which is essential for any insurance claim or lawsuit.

## 8. Notify Your Insurance Company

Report the accident to your insurance company as soon as possible. Be truthful about what happened, but stick to the facts. Don't speculate about fault or the extent of your injuries.

## 9. Don't Accept Quick Settlement Offers

Insurance companies may contact you quickly with a settlement offer. While it might be tempting to accept money right away, these initial offers are often far below what your claim is worth. Once you accept a settlement, you can't go back and ask for more money, even if your injuries turn out to be more serious than initially thought.

## Understanding Maryland's Contributory Negligence Rule

Maryland is one of only a few states that follows a **contributory negligence** rule. This means that if you're found to be even 1% at fault for the accident, you may be barred from recovering any compensation.

This strict rule makes it especially important to:
- Not admit fault at the accident scene
- Document everything thoroughly
- Work with an experienced attorney who understands how to protect your claim

## Time Limits for Filing a Claim

Maryland has a **three-year statute of limitations** for personal injury claims. This means you have three years from the date of the accident to file a lawsuit. While this may seem like plenty of time, evidence can disappear, witnesses' memories fade, and building a strong case takes time. Don't wait to get legal help.
