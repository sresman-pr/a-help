One of the most common questions we hear from potential clients is: "How much does a personal injury lawyer cost?" It's a fair question—especially when you're already dealing with medical bills, lost wages, and the stress of recovering from an injury.

The good news is that most personal injury lawyers, including our firm, work on a **contingency fee basis**. This means you pay nothing upfront, and we only get paid if we win your case.

## What Is a Contingency Fee?

A contingency fee is a payment arrangement where the attorney's fee is "contingent" on the outcome of the case. If we don't recover compensation for you, you don't owe us any attorney fees.

If we do win your case, our fee is a percentage of the amount recovered. This percentage is agreed upon upfront before we begin working on your case, so there are no surprises.

## Typical Contingency Fee Percentages

Contingency fees in personal injury cases typically range from **33% to 40%** of the recovery, depending on several factors:

### Standard Cases (33%)
For most personal injury cases that settle before a lawsuit is filed, the contingency fee is typically around 33% (one-third) of the settlement.

### Cases That Go to Litigation (40%)
If your case requires filing a lawsuit and going through the litigation process, the fee typically increases to around 40%. This reflects the additional time, resources, and risk involved in taking a case to trial.

### Complex Cases
Some particularly complex cases, such as medical malpractice or product liability, may have different fee arrangements due to the extensive investigation and expert witnesses required.

## What About Costs and Expenses?

In addition to attorney fees, there are also **case costs and expenses** that may be incurred during your case, such as:

- **Filing fees** for court documents
- **Expert witness fees** (medical experts, accident reconstructionists, economists)
- **Medical records** and other documentation costs
- **Deposition costs**
- **Investigation expenses**
- **Trial exhibits and presentations**

At our firm, we typically advance these costs and only recover them from your settlement at the end of the case. If we don't win, you typically don't owe us for these costs either—though this should be clarified in your fee agreement.

## Why Contingency Fees Make Sense

The contingency fee system offers several important benefits:

### 1. Access to Justice
Without contingency fees, many injured people couldn't afford to hire a lawyer. The system ensures that everyone has access to quality legal representation, regardless of their financial situation.

### 2. Aligned Interests
Because we only get paid if you get paid, our interests are aligned with yours. We're motivated to get you the highest possible recovery.

### 3. Risk Sharing
We take on the risk of your case. If we invest significant time and resources but don't win, we don't get paid. This means we're selective about the cases we take and confident in those we do.

### 4. No Upfront Costs
You don't have to worry about coming up with money to pay for legal representation when you're already dealing with accident-related expenses.

## Questions to Ask Before Hiring an Attorney

When consulting with personal injury attorneys, ask these questions about fees:

1. **What is your contingency fee percentage?** Get this in writing.

2. **Does the percentage change if the case goes to trial?** Understand how the fee structure works at different stages.

3. **How are costs handled?** Find out if costs are advanced and what happens if the case doesn't succeed.

4. **Are there any expenses I might be responsible for regardless of the outcome?** Clarify this upfront.

5. **Do you negotiate medical liens?** This can significantly impact your final recovery.

6. **What services are included in the fee?** Understand what you're getting for your money.

## Beware of "Too Good to Be True" Offers

Be cautious of attorneys who promise unusually low fees or guarantee specific results. Quality representation requires resources, and attorneys need fair compensation to provide the best possible service.

Similarly, be wary of attorneys who pressure you to sign immediately or don't take time to explain their fee structure clearly.
